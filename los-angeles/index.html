What to Do Before Our Los Angeles Crew Arrives

What you should do before we arrive depends on what kind of water you are looking at. Water from a broken supply line or an overflowing sink is the safest kind. You can walk in it with shoes on, soak it up, and move belongings out of it. Water from a washing machine, dishwasher or aquarium is dirtier, so wear gloves and wash your hands afterward. Anything from a sewer, or water that came in from outside, is hazardous, so shut the room and stay away from it.

Whatever the type, a few things help our Los Angeles crew start faster. Make room for the truck near the entrance and clear the route from the front door to the damage. Write down when you found the water and what you think caused it. Have your insurance details at hand if you can reach them without trouble.

Time matters because clean water does not stay clean. After it has sat in building materials it degrades, and mold can take hold in 24 to 48 hours. That is why the opening hour counts, even when a Los Angeles leak seems small.

Water Damage We See Most in Los Angeles

Water usually escapes at the same few vulnerable spots in a home. The list below covers what we are asked to clean up most for Los Angeles owners.

Water heaters: Sediment collects in the bottom of a tank and slowly eats through the steel. Look for moisture at the base, rust streaks on the jacket or a small pool that keeps coming back. A split tank does not stop at one tankful, because the inlet keeps feeding it until the valve is shut. When the unit lives in an attic or interior closet, the water reaches carpets and ceilings far from it.

Roofs: One strong storm is enough to peel back shingles and push water beneath the roof covering. Leaks also start around chimneys, vent pipes and skylights where the sealant has dried out. Water travels along rafters before it drips, so the wet spot inside may be far from the hole outside. For roof leaks in Los Angeles, we tarp where needed, pull out soaked insulation and dry the structure so the ceiling can be repaired.

Freezing: Depending on the season, a hard overnight freeze can burst lines that were fine the day before. The first to go are hose bibs, irrigation lines and any pipe routed through an unheated space. Vacant homes and rooms with the heat turned down are especially exposed. No flow or a bare trickle at the tap on a frigid day means a frozen line, so close the main and get help.

Backups: Gurgling sounds and more than one slow fixture at once suggest the main drain is partly blocked. Once it closes off fully, anything you flush or drain returns at the lowest opening in the house. The water is unsanitary, and hard surfaces need disinfecting after the solids and liquid are removed. Until a plumber opens the line, do not use sinks or toilets and keep everyone clear of the mess.

Seepage: A wet line along the bottom of a foundation wall is a sign that water is building up in the soil next to it. The usual reasons are grading that tilts inward, clogged perimeter drains and downspouts that empty too close. White chalky deposits, peeling masonry paint and a persistent damp smell are early warnings. The team at Paramount Restoration can extract standing water and dry the space, and we will tell you what we think let it in.

Preventing Water Damage in Los Angeles Homes

Houses tell you when water is getting where it should not. The signs are easy to miss if you do not know them, so here is what our technicians look for in Los Angeles homes. Blistering paint, ceiling stains, warped baseboards and floorboards that curl at the edges each point to water behind or beneath them. Add a stale odor in an enclosed space, a loose toilet, or a faucet that keeps dripping.

Taken singly they are minor, though every one of them gets more expensive with time. Renew the caulk around baths and showers once it splits, because water that slips behind tile stays hidden for a long time. If you have a sump pump, pour a bucket of water into the pit a few times a year to confirm it switches on. In basements and garages, shelving and plastic tubs keep belongings clear of the floor.

Good upkeep lowers the odds, but it cannot remove them. Fewer and smaller leaks are still a real gain, especially compared with a Los Angeles home full of drying machines for most of a week.
