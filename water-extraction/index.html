Before anything else in a restoration project can work, the water has to be extracted. The faster standing water is removed from your property, the less damage occurs to structural materials, flooring, personal belongings and building systems. Every hour that water sits on surfaces, it penetrates deeper into materials, soaking through carpet into padding, wicking up drywall, saturating wood framing and seeping into subflooring. Across Paramount, Paramount Restoration handles emergency extraction with industrial portable and truck-mounted machines that can remove hundreds of gallons a minute.

Truck-mounted units rank with the strongest water removal systems made, and their suction lifts water out of hard floors, carpet and structural materials much better than anything you can rent or buy. Confined spaces, wall cavities and the gaps between floor layers call for different gear, so we use portable extractors, specialty tools and submersible pumps in places the truck-mounted hoses cannot get to.

There is more to a professional extraction than getting rid of the water in plain sight. Water migrates behind baseboards, below flooring, into wall cavities and under cabinets, and our technicians, who are IICRC-certified, locate it with moisture detection tools. Specialty extraction tools remove as much moisture as possible from these hidden areas before the drying phase begins, which significantly reduces the time and equipment needed for structural drying and lowers the risk of secondary mold growth.

Why Water Extraction Is Not a DIY Job

How fast and how completely water is extracted decides how much of your property is damaged and what restoration will cost. Professional truck-mounted extraction equipment removes water hundreds of times faster than consumer tools, so what might take you an entire day with a shop vacuum takes our equipment minutes. This speed difference is not just about convenience, it is about preventing thousands of dollars in additional damage that occurs with every hour water stays in contact with building materials.

Our extraction also goes after moisture that home equipment can neither find nor get to. It follows plumbing lines and electrical conduits, slips between flooring layers, climbs drywall by wicking and is drawn into carpet pad through capillary action. Surface extraction by itself leaves much of this behind, so our team uses specialty techniques and tools aimed at the hidden moisture. This thorough approach reduces drying time, lowers equipment costs and, most importantly, prevents the hidden mold growth that results from trapped moisture.

How to Prevent Water Extraction Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • A sump pump that has not been checked may fail in a storm, so test it ahead of wet weather
  • Put together a simple kit with a bucket, some old towels and the phone number of your restoration company
  • Raise anything you store in the basement a few inches above the floor
  • Make sure your home has working water leak sensors in critical areas
  • Worn or aging hoses on washers, dishwashers and ice makers should be replaced before they fail
  • Install automatic water shutoff valves that detect leaks and stop the flow

Signs It Is Time for Water Extraction

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Ongoing leakage from the plumbing, the air conditioning or an appliance
  • Ceilings, light fixtures or walls that are actively leaking water
  • Standing water on any floor surface, whatever the source
  • Wet baseboards and water creeping from beneath kitchen or bath cabinets
  • Floors flooded by a fire sprinkler or suppression system going off
  • Water entering through a flooded basement stairwell or window well
  • Storm water collecting in the lowest level of the home
