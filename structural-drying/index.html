Of all the stages in restoring water damage, drying the structure counts most, and it is the stage most often botched by people without training. After extraction removes the standing water, significant moisture remains trapped in structural materials, including drywall, wood framing, subflooring, concrete, insulation and other building components. This trapped moisture must be scientifically removed through controlled evaporation and dehumidification to prevent the mold growth, wood rot, material degradation and odor problems that develop when structures remain wet.

Paramount Restoration employs IICRC-certified structural drying technicians who understand psychrometry, the study of temperature, humidity and moisture relationships in air. For each job they apply it to calculate airflow patterns, temperature settings and the placement of equipment that will give the best conditions for evaporation. Because hardwood, drywall, plaster and concrete do not dry at the same speed, the method has to change with the material to bring it to acceptable moisture content without harming it further.

Every day of the drying process, we check moisture using thermal imaging cameras, thermo-hygrometers and calibrated moisture meters. The result is a detailed log of readings that tracks how moisture comes down over time in each material that got wet. Those readings guide daily equipment adjustments that keep the drying as efficient as it can be. Drying is complete only when all affected materials reach acceptable moisture standards as defined by IICRC S500 guidelines, not when surfaces simply feel dry to the touch.

Why Professional Structural Drying Matters

Drying a structure properly takes science, and guesswork has no place in it. The leading cause of secondary mold after a water event is setting out a few fans and trusting they will be enough. Professional structural drying requires understanding how different materials absorb and release moisture, calculating the grain depression (moisture removal capacity) each situation needs, and monitoring daily to confirm drying is progressing properly. Without this knowledge, under-drying leaves moisture that feeds mold, while over-drying can damage wood and other materials.

Professional drying equipment is not a bigger version of what stores sell, it is a different kind of machine. Commercial LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers remove 10 to 20 times more moisture from the air than residential dehumidifiers, and high-velocity air movers create the precise airflow patterns needed to accelerate evaporation from structural surfaces. Machines like these cost a great deal and take training to use well, which explains why professional results are so much better than a do-it-yourself effort.

How to Tell You Need Structural Drying

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Materials breaking down in a spot that got wet before and was not dried by professionals
  • Swollen trim and baseboards separating at the seams
  • Condensation forming on windows in the area that was wet
  • After water damage, a meter shows elevated moisture in the floor, wall or ceiling
  • Stained, swollen or soft spots in the drywall
  • No visible water left, yet walls or floors still feel cold and damp
  • High humidity in affected areas despite efforts to ventilate

Structural Drying, Step by Step

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's how we handle structural drying from start to finish:

1

Mapping the Moisture

We build a complete moisture map of every affected area with hygrometers, thermal imaging and moisture meters. Baseline readings come from the map, and so does the plan for placing equipment.

2

Equipment Deployment

Guided by the moisture map, we place high-velocity air movers and commercial LGR dehumidifiers where they will do the most good. We tune airflow and temperature to suit the materials that need drying.

Our technician returns daily to take moisture readings at all monitoring points, adjust equipment placement and document drying progress. We keep you posted on how the timeline looks.

How to Prevent Structural Drying Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • The dehumidifiers and air movers should run around the clock until our technician takes them out
  • If a new damp spot or smell shows up while drying is underway, let the technician know at once
  • Go by the meter, not by touch, since a dry-feeling surface may still hold moisture deep inside
  • Dehumidifiers lose efficiency when outside air gets in, so keep drying areas closed up
  • Deal with water intrusion right away, since an early start on drying gives a better result
  • Plan for daily access so readings can be taken and equipment adjusted
